**Integral Calculation and Area Interpretation**

**1. Definite Integral Calculation:**
$$ \int_{-3}^{3} (x+1) dx = \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} + x + C \right]_{-3}^{3} $$
$$ = \left( \frac{3^2}{2} + 3 \right) - \left( \frac{(-3)^2}{2} + (-3) \right) $$
$$ = \left( \frac{9}{2} + 3 \right) - \left( \frac{9}{2} - 3 \right) $$
$$ = \left( \frac{9}{2} + \frac{6}{2} \right) - \left( \frac{9}{2} - \frac{6}{2} \right) $$
$$ = \frac{15}{2} - \frac{3}{2} = \frac{12}{2} = 6 $$

**2. Geometric Interpretation (Area under the curve $f(x)=x+1$):**
The graph shows two areas:
*   **Area 1 (A1):** Above the x-axis, from x=-1 to x=3. This is a triangle with base 4 and height 4.
    *   Area_1 = $\frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 4 = 8$
*   **Area 2 (A2):** Below the x-axis, from x=-3 to x=-1. This is a triangle with base 2 and height 2.
    *   Area_2 = $-\frac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 2 = -2$ (negative because it's below the x-axis)

The area of the 3rd quartile will be negative.
Therefore, my integral $A_1 + A_2 = 8 - 2 = 6$.

**3. Understanding "Area" vs. "Integral":**
However, I could be asked to calculate "the area" (not the integral).
*   Negative areas must be turned to positive.
*   $|A_1| + |A_2| = |8| + |-2| = 10$

**4. Integral Properties for Calculating Area:**
Remember integrals are sums, hence:
$$ \int_{-3}^{3} (x+1) dx = \int_{-3}^{-1} (x+1) dx + \int_{-1}^{3} (x+1) dx $$

If I were to calculate the area, I could divide the integral into a sum, and divide it exactly where it becomes negative, where $f(x)=0$.

**5. Calculating Absolute Area:**
Now, the negative area is turned to positive to find the area with absolute value.
$$ \int_{-3}^{3} (x+1) dx = \left| \int_{-3}^{-1} (x+1) dx \right| + \int_{-1}^{3} (x+1) dx $$

The negative Area can also be made positive by using the last integral property:
$$ \int_{-4}^{-2} f(x) dx = - \int_{-2}^{-4} f(x) dx $$
