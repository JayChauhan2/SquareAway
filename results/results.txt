**Estimating Area Between Curves**

**Unit 8**
**Date: Nov 3, 2022**

We can calculate the area between curves by estimating with rectangles and taking the limit to get the definite Integral.

**Example: Area between y = x² and y = 2-x**
*   The region of interest is bounded by the curves y = x² and y = 2-x.
*   The intersection points appear to be at x = -2 and x = 1.5.
*   **Height** of a rectangle in the region = (Upper curve) - (Lower curve) = (2-x) - x²
*   **Width** of each rectangle (ΔX) = (1.5 - (-2)) / n = 3.5 / n, where 'n' is the number of rectangles.
*   **Riemann Sum Approximation:**
    Σ (from i=1 to n) [ (2 - Xᵢ₋₁) - Xᵢ₋² ] ΔX
*   **Definite Integral:**
    ∫ (from -2 to 1.5) [ (2-x) - x² ] dx

**Negative Integral Values**

When integrating a function, the integral represents the signed area between the curve and the x-axis.
*   Areas above the x-axis are positive.
*   Areas below the x-axis are negative.

**Example: ∫ (from a to b) F(t) dt**
If the graph of F(t) has sections labeled A, B, C, D, E (where A, C, E are above the x-axis and B, D are below):
∫ (from a to b) F(t) dt = A + C + E - B - D

**Midpoint Rule**

*   **Left(n)** and **Right(n)** Riemann sums for approximating definite integrals are often inefficient and crude estimates.
*   To achieve a more accurate and efficient approximation, the **Midpoint Rule** is used.

**Comparison of Approximation Methods:**

*   **Left Riemann Sum:** Rectangles use the height of the function at the left endpoint of each subinterval. (Often an underestimate for increasing functions, overestimate for decreasing functions).
*   **Right Riemann Sum:** Rectangles use the height of the function at the right endpoint of each subinterval. (Often an overestimate for increasing functions, underestimate for decreasing functions).
*   **Midpoint Riemann Sum:** Rectangles use the height of the function at the midpoint of each subinterval.
    *   Errors often **cancel out**, leading to a more accurate estimate for general functions.
    *   For **linear functions**, the Midpoint Rule provides a value **exactly equal to the definite integral**.
