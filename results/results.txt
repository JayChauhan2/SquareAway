**Formatted Notes:**

**Problem:** Evaluate the definite integral:
$$ \int_{1/2}^{8} \left(2 - \frac{1}{x}\right) dx $$

**Step 1: Find the antiderivative of the integrand.**
The antiderivative of $2$ is $2x$.
The antiderivative of $-\frac{1}{x}$ is $-\ln|x|$.
Since the interval of integration ($1/2$ to $8$) only contains positive values, we can write $-\ln x$.

Thus, the antiderivative is:
$$ [2x - \ln x] $$

**Step 2: Apply the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.**
Evaluate the antiderivative at the upper limit ($8$) and subtract the evaluation at the lower limit ($1/2$).

$$ [2x - \ln x]_{1/2}^{8} = (2 \cdot 8 - \ln 8) - \left(2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} - \ln \frac{1}{2}\right) $$
$$ = (16 - \ln 8) - (1 - \ln 0.5) $$
$$ = 16 - \ln 8 - 1 + \ln 0.5 $$
$$ = 15 - \ln 8 + \ln 0.5 $$

**Step 3: Simplify the logarithmic terms.**
We know that $\ln 0.5 = \ln \left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = -\ln 2$.

Substitute this into the expression:
$$ 15 - \ln 8 + (-\ln 2) $$
$$ = 15 - \ln 8 - \ln 2 $$

**Step 4: Combine the logarithmic terms.**
Using the logarithm property $\ln a + \ln b = \ln(ab)$:
$$ = 15 - (\ln 8 + \ln 2) $$
$$ = 15 - \ln (8 \cdot 2) $$
$$ = 15 - \ln 16 $$

**Final Answer:**
$$ 15 - \ln 16 $$
